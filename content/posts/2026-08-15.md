In the last part of this series, we simulated a point mass making frictional contact with the ground plane.
This time, we'll simulate a 3-dimensional cube doing the same thing.

But let's start by making it float in a zero-G environment.

To simulate a three-dimensional rigid body, 
our new state-space system must encapsulate not just the object's position and linear velocity,
but also its orientation/attitude and angular velocity.
That leads us to the following state vector:

{{< katex >}} 
$$
\begin{equation}
X = \begin{bmatrix} r_W \\ q \\ v_W \\ \omega_B \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
$$
{{< /katex >}}

Where $r_W$ is the body's world-frame position, $q$ is the body's attitude quaternion,
$v_W$ is the body's world-frame linear velocity,
and $\omega_B$ is the body-frame angular velocity
(why not world-frame angular velocity?).
(also mention why quaternions.)

As before, we need to formulate the state vector's derivative $\dot{X}$ in order to time-step.
This starts out easy: the derivative of $r_W$ is just $v_W$. 

Differentiating the attitude quaternion is a bit trickier; it requires the following transformation to be applied, where $\otimes$ represents a quaternion multiplication operation. I won't get into the derivation of that here because that could take up an entire blog post by itself. If you're curious, look to section 4.5 of [^1].

{{< katex >}} 
$$
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q} = \frac{1}{2} q \otimes 
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\
        \omega_B \\
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
$$
{{< /katex >}}

For convenience, we can express the quaternion multiplication operation as a matrix operation,
using $L(q)$ as defined in [^2] and [^3].

{{< katex >}} 
$$
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q} =\frac{1}{2}L(q)H\omega_B
\end{equation}
$$
{{< /katex >}}

{{< katex >}} 
$$
\begin{equation}
    F_W = m \dot{v}_W
\end{equation}
$$
$$
\begin{equation}
    \tau_B = J \dot{\omega}_B + \omega_B \times J \omega_B
\end{equation}
$$
{{< /katex >}}

{{< katex >}} 
$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{r}_W \\ \dot{q} \\ \dot{v}_W \\ \dot{\omega}_B \end{bmatrix} = 
\begin{bmatrix}
	v_W \\
    \frac{1}{2}L(q)H\omega_B \\
    \frac{1}{m}F_B \\
    J^{-1}(\tau_B + \omega_B \times J \omega_B) \\
\end{bmatrix}

\end{equation}
$$
{{< /katex >}}

simulate a 3D objects, we'll use the Special Euclidean Group, SE(3).
